                                 INTRODUCTION

1.1 Background

Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) has become one of the most widely used di-
agnostic modalities in modern medicine due to its superior soft-tissue contrast and
non-ionizing nature. However, the strong static magnetic field, rapidly switching gra-
dient fields, and radiofrequency (RF) pulses used during an MRI scan pose serious
safety risks to patients carrying implantable electronic devices--such as pacemakers,
implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs), neurostimulators, cochlear implants, in-
sulin pumps, and implantable loop recorders. Depending on the device's physical com-
position and MRI conditionality status, unverified exposure to magnetic fields can in-
duce electric currents, lead heating, tissue damage, altered device function, or mechan-
ical displacement.
Before performing an MRI procedure, radiologists are legally and clinically required to
verify whether a patient has an implanted device and determine its exact manufacturer
and model. MRI safety status (MR Safe, MR Conditional, or MR Unsafe) varies signif-
icantly even between different model iterations produced by the same manufacturer. In
clinical practice, this information is ideally retrieved from patient implant identification
cards or electronic health records (EHR). However, documentation is frequently miss-
ing--cards are lost, patient records across institutions are fragmented, or emergency
patients may be unresponsive. In these critical scenarios, chest X-rays are routinely
used as a fallback to visually detect the presence of implantable electronic devices.

1.2 Problem Statement

Missing documentation and fragmented patient records require manual X-ray checks
for implantable electronic devices. Accidental or unverified exposure to MRI fields can
lead to catastrophic device failures and severe patient risks. Manual visual identification
on chest radiographs is slow, error-prone, and heavily reliant on specialized radiologist
expertise.

1.3 Objectives
The key objectives of this project are:

   1. To design and train a deep learning model for automated bounding-box localiza-
       tion of implantable electronic devices (IEDs) on standard chest radiographs.

   2. To develop a fine-grained classification model that identifies the exact manufac-
       turer and model of the localized device from the cropped X-ray region.

   3. To build a multi-task safety-profiling module that maps the identified device to
       its corresponding MRI safety category (MR Safe / MR Conditional / MR Unsafe)
       and generates an instant compatibility report for clinical use.

1.4 Application Areas
The proposed computer vision framework is applicable across critical healthcare do-
mains:

    · Radiology & Medical Imaging
    · Emergency Medicine
    · Clinical Decision Support Systems (CDSS)
    · Hospital Administration

                            LITERATURE REVIEW

The automated localization and fine-grained classification of Implantable Electronic
Devices (IEDs)--such as pacemakers, implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs),
neurostimulators, cochlear implants, and insulin pumps--on chest radiographs have
emerged as vital clinical safeguards against accidental MRI exposure. This chapter
reviews the literature on artificial intelligence (AI) for implantable device identification,
MRI safety assessment, and clinical decision support integration.

2.1 Deep Learning for CIED Localization and Fine-Grained Identification

Early machine learning studies by Melendez et al. [1] demonstrated the feasibility of
classifying cardiac devices from X-ray visual features. Subsequent deep learning ad-
vancements significantly improved detection accuracy and safety characterization. Kim
et al. [2] introduced CNN algorithms to detect CIEDs and map their MRI safety pro-
files directly from chest radiographs. Theriault Lauzier et al. [3] further applied semi-
supervised AI methods across large radiograph datasets to tackle medical label scarcity.
Recent research by Busch et al. [4] showed that deep learning models achieve high ac-
curacy in identifying cardiac device manufacturers and models across standard DICOM
and smartphone-captured radiographs. Additionally, foreign object detection studies by
Kufel et al. [5] highlighted the necessity of automated bounding box localization to
isolate medical implants from background anatomical structures.

2.2 MRI Safety, Electromagnetic Field Interactions, and Labeling

Accidental MRI exposure of unverified CIEDs poses critical patient risks, including
lead heating, gradient-induced currents, and altered pacing modes. Establishing ex-
act device manufacturer and model identity is essential to determine MR Conditional
parameters.
Oh et al. [6] examined MRI safety challenges in ultra-high field systems (3.0T and
beyond), emphasizing national clinical safety directives. Brown et al. [7] utilized com-

                                                   3
putational modeling and electromagnetic simulations to evaluate MR Conditional safety
for active implantable devices. Furthermore, Zanovello et al. [8] demonstrated that si-
multaneous exposure to RF and gradient fields heavily influences MR safety labeling,
reinforcing the need for model-level classification.

2.3 Clinical Workflow Integration and Pre-Deployment Assessment

Translating vision models into hospital workflows requires pre-deployment assessment.
White et al. [9] evaluated AI models assisting radiologists in identifying lead-less and
traditional electronic implants for pre-MRI screening, establishing operational guide-
lines for PACS and Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) integration. Broader
reviews by Gherghe et al. [10] support multi-task AI frameworks that combine spatial
localization, classification, and risk prediction.

2.4 Research Gaps and Proposed System Contribution

Despite recent progress, existing literature exhibits key limitations:
   1. Isolated Task Processing: Existing models separate localization, fine-grained
       classification, and MRI safety lookup rather than unifying them into an automated
       pipeline.
   2. Coarse Classification Granularity: Many approaches fail to identify specific
       device model lines required for exact MR Conditional protocol verification.
   3. Absence of Instant Profiling: Few frameworks offer real-time clinical dash-
       boards with color-coded safety badges and field strength limits.

Our proposed system directly addresses these gaps by implementing an end-to-end
framework combining automated bounding box localization, fine-grained classification,
and multi-task MRI safety profiling.

                                METHODOLOGY

3.1 System Design Architecture and Overview
The proposed computer vision framework for Implantable Electronic Device (IED)
identification and MRI safety profiling consists of four interconnected core layers:

   1. Data Ingestion & Preprocessing Pipeline: Accepts standard chest radiographs
       (DICOM files, high-resolution PNG/JPEG images), applies Contrast Limited Adap-
       tive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE), normalizes intensity, and formats tensors
       for model processing.

   2. Automated Bounding Box Localization Module: Utilizes deep object detection
       models (YOLOv8 / YOLOv11) to isolate device regions of interest (ROIs) on full
       chest scans and extract cropped device patches.

   3. Fine-Grained Classification Module: Employs deep convolutional and vision
       transformer architectures (EfficientNet-B4 / Swin Transformer) to predict exact
       device manufacturers (Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Abbott, Biotronik) and spe-
       cific model families.

   4. Multi-Task MRI Safety Profiler: Queries a structured clinical lookup database
       and safety rule engine to output MR conditionality status (MR Safe, MR Con-
       ditional, MR Unsafe), static magnetic field limits (1.5T / 3.0T), SAR limits, and
       operational warnings.

The system workflow operates through four sequential processing stages: (1) radio-
graph ingestion and CLAHE quality enhancement, (2) deep bounding box localization
predicting coordinates [xmin, ymin, xmax, ymax], (3) fine-grained classification predicting
manufacturer and model series, and (4) multi-task safety profiling mapping predictions
to clinical Instructions for Use (IFU) databases for color-coded status badges, field re-
strictions (1.5T/3.0T), and SAR thresholds.

3.2 Data Collection, Preprocessing, and Pipeline Architecture

Chest radiograph datasets containing verified pacemakers, ICDs, CRTs, and loop recorders
are aggregated from multi-center repositories (MIMIC-CXR, NIH ChestX-ray14, PACS
archives) and specialized CIED benchmarks. Device MR Conditional protocols and
field limits are compiled from official manufacturer IFUs. Preprocessing normalizes
dynamic ranges and applies CLAHE to highlight subtle metallic lead and header struc-
tures. Bounding box ground truth coordinates [xmin, ymin, xmax, ymax] are structured in

                                                   6
YOLO format with augmentations (±10 rotation, scaling, intensity jittering).

3.3 Core Detection and Safety Profiling Modules

Automated Bounding Box Localization: The single-stage detection network (YOLO)
isolates the device ROI from full chest scans using a composite loss function combining
CIoU bounding box regression and cross-entropy classification loss:

Ldetection = boxLCIoU + clsLBCE                                               (3.1)

The predicted bounding box extracts a cropped high-resolution device patch IROI.

Fine-Grained Classification & Safety Profiling: The ROI patch IROI is fed into fine-
tuned classifiers (EfficientNet-B4 / Swin Transformer) to analyze header transparency,
battery shape, and lead port geometry, yielding probability distributions P(Ymanufacturer,Ymodel |
IROI). Predictions are mapped to verified IFU tables to output MR categorization (MR
Safe, MR Conditional, MR Unsafe), field limits (1.5T vs. 3.0T), SAR caps, and pacing
mode requirements.

                EXPECTED RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The proposed computer vision framework for Implantable Electronic Device (IED)
identification and MRI safety profiling is expected to deliver an automated, real-time
pre-screening tool for chest radiographs. By eliminating reliance on missing implant
cards or delayed medical records, the system aims to streamline pre-MRI screening in
emergency medicine and radiology workflows.
The framework is expected to achieve robust performance across three core dimensions:

   1. Automated Bounding Box Localization: Object detection models (YOLO) will
       precisely locate pacemakers, ICDs, CRT devices, and loop recorders, extracting
       ROI patches without spatial clipping.

   2. Fine-Grained Model Identification: Deep classifiers (EfficientNet / Swin Trans-
       former) will distinguish header geometries and lead configurations to identify ma-
       jor manufacturers (Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Abbott, Biotronik, LivaNova)
       and specific model lines.

   3. Multi-Task MRI Safety Profiling: The rule engine will map predictions to clin-
       ical IFU databases, generating color-coded MR conditionality badges (MR Safe,
       MR Conditional, MR Unsafe) and operational field limits (1.5T / 3.0T).

Integrating automated vision models into hospital PACS and Clinical Decision Support
Systems (CDSS) provides instant visual overlays for radiologists. By replacing manual
X-ray inspection with automated bounding box identification, the framework prevents
magnetic displacement and RF heating risks in trauma patients, establishing a practical
AI safeguard that reduces pre-MRI screening delays while enhancing patient safety.
