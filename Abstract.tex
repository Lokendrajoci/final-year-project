\chapter*{ABSTRACT}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{ABSTRACT}

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) extend the capabilities of large language models by enabling joint reasoning over text and images, but this multimodal capability also introduces new safety vulnerabilities. Safety alignment techniques such as RLHF and DPO provide only a soft, learned barrier against harmful behavior, and this barrier can be bypassed through jailbreak attacks. In VLMs, the visual modality significantly expands the attack surface, enabling threats such as typographic injection, where harmful instructions are embedded as text within an image; query-relevant pairing, where individually harmless text and image inputs combine to produce harmful intent; and continuous-space adversarial perturbations, where imperceptible pixel-level changes suppress the model's refusal behavior. Existing defenses are limited: text-only guardrails cannot inspect visual content, while internal representation-monitoring methods require white-box access and incur high computational overhead, making them impractical for black-box, API-level deployment.

This project proposes LITMUS, a lightweight, model-agnostic multimodal guardrail that operates at the input level, before requests reach a downstream VLM. The system employs a tri-branch architecture that independently encodes raw image features, OCR-extracted text from the image, and the original user text. These modality-specific representations are projected into a shared feature space, combined through a modality-gating mechanism, and fused via a multilayer perceptron to produce a jailbreak risk score. Calibrated thresholds then classify each input as Allow or Block, permitting safe requests to proceed to the protected VLM while rejecting potentially malicious ones. The guardrail's effectiveness will be evaluated against the three targeted attack types using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and false-positive rate, with the goal of delivering a computationally efficient defense that improves the safety of vision-language systems without requiring access to the underlying model's internals.
